This is a retrospective audit of existing artifacts, not preregistration or new model evidence. It used standard-library JSON, arithmetic, text inspection and corpus reads only; no model/tokenizer job, torch import, checkpoint load, or historical-artifact modification. A separately running B28last16 process was left alone. The parent independently verified implementation digest `a329eb7bb06fc003a9220ea41713e721fba575c478008240749c425774b2b58e`; every audited result references that same digest. This audit does not claim to requalify the gate ladder.

Inputs include the user goal attachment and its required pasted-text attachment, root `CONTINUATION.md`, `experiments/architecture_benchmark_001/AGENTS.md`, `protocol.json` G1_RETENTION, and `evidence/retention-design-v1/DISTINGUISHABILITY_PREREG.md`. ## Hlast4: real permissive PASS, overstated mechanism

`E/ARM_DOSE_Hlast4_lr1e-05_b5200_seed17_wsd0.2-0.2_b20.95.json` confirms 49 correct, 47/47 retained, 0 lost, 149 terminated, and 29 output texts changed from source. Per-class retained counts are 21/21 percentage, 16/16 ratio, and 10/10 time. This is a real observed PASS under the registered permissive policy. It opens no official gate or efficacy license: the artifact itself is exploratory.

The two gains split as follows:

- `rineya_v3_sft__percentage_reasoning__reas_percentage_reasoning_s08__02515`: format_only.
- `rineya_v3_sft__time_duration__reas_time_duration_s05__01696`: wrong_answer.

Only one of these two rows is a capability-stratum acquisition under A6. Against source, the total-correctness discordance is 2-0 (exact two-sided p=.5), so the gain is not statistically demonstrated.

The zero-loss statement needs its policy qualifier. The same artifact's strict sensitivity result FAILS: 42 correct, 40/41 retained, 1 lost, 149 terminated. The lost strict row is `rineya_v3_sft__ratio_proportion__reas_ratio_proportion_s05__01698`. This does not overturn the registered permissive PASS, but makes an unrestricted claim of zero loss under every scoring convention false.

Hlast4 and B28last8 agree on correctness for 146/150 rows; they agree on exact final output text (and complete stored output record) on only 70/150. Eighty output texts differ. The goal's “behaviourally almost the same model” statement incorrectly turns correctness agreement into response agreement.

The norm arithmetic is real, but does not identify a mechanism. `arm_dose.py::branch_norm` computes sqrt(sum of squared elements across trainable read output projections). Hlast4's norm is .4409782290 across 4 tensors; H WSD's is .5273331404 across 28. Dividing by sqrt(tensor count) gives RMS per-tensor Frobenius norms .2204891145 and .09965659625 (ratio 2.21249). These are RMS averages, not measurements showing that EACH surviving read is 2.2 times stronger. Weight norm also is not branch-activation magnitude. The comparison changes active-site count, site positions, trainable parameter count (18,880,128 vs 119,571,072), and optimization dynamics; it does not hold total perturbation or activation strength fixed. “Simultaneity confirmed,” “the destructive variable is number of sites, not strength,” and “under-trained, not over-trained” are hypotheses unsupported as causal conclusions by one dose/seed. Observed zero loss on 47 reference rows does not guarantee zero loss elsewhere or after additional training.

B28 full's 41/47 retention is lower than Hlast4's 47/47. Consequently “B28 dominates EVERY CED arm on both axes” is false once Hlast4 is included. It descriptively dominates the measured full-read H/P arms; Hlast4 trades much lower total score for higher observed retention.

## Paired tests and the missing multiplicity correction

All six raw p-values in the current `E/ARM_COMPARISON.json` were independently recomputed from the saved 150-row correctness maps. Its p-values are arithmetically correct. The preregistration explicitly says additional comparisons require a correction; `arm_compare.py` nevertheless applies p<.05 separately to all six current comparisons and the JSON calls P WSD vs H WSD significant.

The following Holm correction treats those six comparisons as one family:

| Current comparison | A-only/B-only correct | Raw exact p | Holm adjusted p |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| P WSD vs H WSD | 23-8 | 0.0106738405302 | 0.0640430431813 |
| Hlast4 WSD vs H WSD | 26-14 | 0.080690467752 | 0.40345233876 |
| H WSD vs B28last8 cosine | 14-24 | 0.143306654296 | 0.573226617184 |
| P WSD vs B28last8 cosine | 20-15 | 0.499559833203 | 1 |
| Hlast4 WSD vs B28last8 cosine | 3-1 | 0.625 | 1 |
| Hlast4 WSD vs P WSD | 16-19 | 0.735878800857 | 1 |

No comparison in this current six-pair WSD/Hlast4/control family survives Holm at .05. P WSD vs H WSD is nominally significant only; its six-test Bonferroni p is also .0640430431813. This correction is retrospective and descriptive: choosing a family now cannot create prospective preregistration, and this six-test family does not include every historical/exploratory comparison ever inspected. Historical cosine contrasts remain separate evidence, not erased by this statement.

The cosine H-vs-B28last8 counts quoted in CONTINUATION are correct (5 vs 36), but the stated p=5.9e-7 is wrong: recomputation gives `7.84157236921601e-7`. H cosine vs P cosine, 5 vs 37, gives `4.4337048166198656e-7` as quoted. P cosine vs B28last8, 14 vs 13, gives 1.0.

“Four orders of magnitude in p proves most damage was the optimizer” overstates attribution. Schedule and beta2 changed together, without repeated runs or a factorial intervention. A difference between p-values is not itself an effect-size decomposition. The descriptive recovery in H score from 16 to 37 is supported.

## Historical B28 73/150 and determinism

`E/RETENTION_DOSE_gpu_lr1e-05_b5200_seed17.json` confirms completion at 5,233 tokens/41 steps, 811.13 wall seconds, CUDA train/CPU probe, TF32 off/off, highest precision, matching digest, and reported initial device control PASS. Its final permissive aggregate is 73 correct, 41 retained, 6 lost, 150 terminated. Per-class gains are 8 percentage +7 ratio +17 time =32; retained counts are 20+15+6=41. Strict sensitivity is 61 correct and 33/41 retained. The historical artifact has no final per-row output/correctness record, so its 32 gains cannot be fully split or paired with other models. Its seven ratio gains are at least known to be in the wrong_answer stratum because that class has no format_only/runaway headroom.

At audit time there is no B28-full checkpoint directory; saved checkpoint manifests/weight files exist for B28last8, H WSD, P WSD, and Hlast4 WSD. File presence/metadata were inspected; tensor contents and SHA256 of weight files were not verified by this audit.

A rerun matching 73/150 would establish agreement of that aggregate only. It would NOT establish identical row outcomes, tensors, or training determinism; even exact historical row identity cannot be checked because that record was not saved. Root CONTINUATION's instruction “if 73 exactly, determinism holds and regenerated weights are the model that scored 73” is invalid. A rerun should report aggregate reproduction with that limitation.

The P cosine milestone claim is supported: correct/retained are 38/24 at 1,397 tokens, 50/37 at 2,610, 49/36 at 3,998, and 48/34 at 5,233. This is a sampled interior maximum, not a proven optimal stopping dose.

## The on-panel split really excludes the monitor panel

A standard-library JSON comparison found `data/monitor_probe.jsonl` and `outputs/splits_large/task_probe.jsonl` exactly equal as decoded row lists: 150 rows, IDs and families, 50 per class. Replaying `build_splits_onpanel.py`'s sorted family list and seed-17 shuffle against the current corpus exactly reproduces these 150 panel families.

The three corpus files contain 1,125 families /7,500 rows. Excluding the panel leaves 975 families /6,492 rows. Excluding calibration/pilot then leaves 965 clean families /6,416 rows. Default builder assignment would select 845 train families and 120 dev families, sampling one row per family; overlaps with the monitor panel are zero for both. The 6,492 figure is the raw spare pool, not the emitted training-row count or the post-reservation pool size.

Existing `outputs/splits_large/train.jsonl` has 10,000 rows and dev has 150; each uses 36 classes with zero rows in the three monitor classes and zero monitor-family overlap. The 73 result is thus transfer across reply classes, as described. `outputs/splits_onpanel` does not exist at audit time. This audit did not import or execute the builder, load a tokenizer, or create those splits.

## Interpolation interpretation and checkpoint metadata

Scaling ONLY the trained read `o_proj` weights by alpha while holding other trained weights fixed gives the intended per-site post-hoc output scaling, with alpha=0 suppressing every read. This does not mean the whole network response or loss scales linearly: later states depend on earlier injections. Interpolating ALL trained parameters toward their initialized values is a different operation because `freeze_backbones()` trains memory and non-output read projections too. A source zero output projection alone does not make full weight interpolation identical to output gating. The R5 implementation/report must distinguish these interventions.

Checkpoint `soup_key.source_digest` in current manifests equals the implementation digest `a329...`; `arm_dose.py` uses `frozen.get('source_state_sha256') or implementation_digest()`. The observed field must not be described as an independently verified source-weight hash. Shape/name compatibility by itself does not establish correct weight provenance. New metadata can state source-asset identity explicitly without changing historical manifests.
