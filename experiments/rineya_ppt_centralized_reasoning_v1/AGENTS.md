## Evidence and execution boundaries

The founding registration is descriptive. It grants no native-run, training,
paid-service, dataset-generation, model-publication, or protected-bank access.
Do not import another experiment's launcher merely to inspect this package.
Do not lower memory, numerical, provenance, or permission gates.

A later implementation must separately qualify transition probabilities,
fixed-horizon/EOS behavior, exact checkpoint log-probabilities, cache behavior,
budget accounting, label isolation, and failure reporting. CPU registration
checks are not CPU sampler tests. CPU sampler tests are not native model gains.

Maintain separate candidate coverage and returned-answer accuracy. Evaluation
labels must not enter proposal acceptance, swaps, routing, stopping, or
label-blind selection. Hold verifier access fixed across comparison arms.
Record all proposals and failures, including rejected work and timeouts.

## Handoff discipline

Keep implementation isolated here unless a separately reviewed integration
expands scope. Before a launch, pin code, checkpoint and tokenizer hashes,
precision, prompts, public split definitions, verifier version, resource
limits, and current launch authorization. Keep the founding registration
historical; add a dated version for a new approved protocol rather than
silently rewriting the original intent or promoting its status.

Report exactly what was implemented, tested, executed, and measured. Claim
neither faithful paper reproduction nor Rineya benefit without the corresponding
evidence. Preserve all inherited files and original experiment identities.
