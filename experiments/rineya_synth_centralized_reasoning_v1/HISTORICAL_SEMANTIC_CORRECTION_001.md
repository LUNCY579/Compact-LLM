# Historical exact-match gains can hide preservation failures

A separate historical OPEN MQ review supplied an
[exposed five-case semantic diagnostic](evidence/historical_semantic_correction_20261005/MQ_SEMANTIC_BASELINE_DAMAGE_004.json).
Two base answers are correct under their public questions although the old
exact-match flags mark them incorrect. One says that two notes both describing
an amber badge do not conflict. The response-only candidate changes this to an
incorrect affirmative answer in all three recorded orders; the whole-sequence
candidate retains the correct negative answer. Numerical repairs on other
cases do not cancel that loss. Root inspected the public questions, arithmetic
and diagnostic assertions; this is not a new native evaluation or qualified
semantic grader.

The [formal witness report](evidence/historical_semantic_correction_20261005/PUBLIC_FORMAL_WITNESSES_001.json)
also illustrates a missing premise: a rule about sparrows inside an aviary plus
a statement that a particular bird is a sparrow does not establish that the
bird is inside that aviary. This formal consequence depends on the explicitly
chosen premises and does not settle every pragmatic reading.

These reports correct interpretation of historical exposed diagnostics only.
They do not compare current SYNTH M1/M2, establish transfer or certify retention.
Historical outputs and scores stay unchanged; no exposed case enters training
or prospective guard enrollment. The frozen student, corpus and recipe stay
unchanged. Current finite R/S verification and predeclared output validity must
remain separate from source-correct guard enrollment and candidate outcomes;
language grading authority remains unresolved.
