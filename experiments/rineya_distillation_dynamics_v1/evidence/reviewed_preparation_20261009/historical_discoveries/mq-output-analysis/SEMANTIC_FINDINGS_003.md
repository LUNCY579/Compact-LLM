advantage on the five directly verifiable questions. It also hides a repeated
loss of source-correct behavior. This is a retrospective exposed DEV diagnosis;
the original grader, native scores, outputs and registrations remain unchanged.

the historical reference. The 14-row subset contains five directly verified
questions, four assumption-dependent questions and five underspecified questions.

The semantic M0 baseline has two directly verified successes: selecting B and
answering that identical amber notes do not conflict. Both were marked wrong by
the historical exact grader. Response retains one and loses one in each order;
whole retains both and loses neither on this small exposed subset. Repairs
cannot cancel the compatible-NO damage. This does not enroll new prospective
retention guards or certify broad retention.

Several historical grades are misleading:

- `B.` and `Option B.` choose the same unique valid radio pack. The question
  requires no particular phrase, so the two historical paired gains are purely
  differences in exact-string acceptance.
- `72 units.` is the correct remaining budget. The old accepted strings were
  `72` and `72.`, hiding two valid numeric successes.
- `No. Both notes say amber.` is correct. The old reference required
  `No, they can both be true.`, hiding whole's success and response's repeated
  destruction of M0's already correct NO answer.

The aviary question is not a valid positive implication as written. Its premises
are “every sparrow **in this aviary** has a band” and “this bird is a sparrow.”
Membership in this aviary is missing. A banded bird inside and an unbanded bird
outside both satisfy the premises. The recorded gold YES is not entailed;
bare NO is not entailed either. Neither archived arm gives the explicit
insufficiency statement requested by the system.

The sequences 10,12,14,16 and 3,5,7,9 admit 18 and 11 under the ordinary arithmetic
progression convention, but no continuation rule is stated. Many rules fit four
terms. Amber versus green conflicts only if the same badge property, time and
aspect are single-valued and the colors mutually exclude. Those assumptions are
plausible intentions, not supplied constraints.

Four roughly-$7 tickets yield a nominal $28 estimate. The old exact reference
`About $30.` chooses an unspecified rounding convention, rejecting reasonable
`$28.` and `About $28.` outputs. The loose-cable question supports a plausible
physical diagnosis but does not identify a unique causal mechanism. Natural
closures and follow-up replies admit multiple valid formulations; arbitrary
exact phrases do not establish semantic or dialogue quality.

The stable whole-better negative asks whether 600 minutes to read a short email
is plausible. NO is sensible under ordinary reading assumptions (600 minutes is
10 hours), but length, reader and circumstances are unspecified. Preserve it as
a convention-dependent negative rather than a formal theorem.
